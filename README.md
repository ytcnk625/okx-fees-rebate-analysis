This isn't an OKX-specific issue — it's how perpetuals work on every exchange — but it's worth understanding before you treat the 0.02% maker fee as the full cost of a leveraged position.

## Proof of Reserves: what it actually proves (and what it doesn't)

OKX publishes a monthly Proof of Reserves report — the 46th edition was released in August 2026, showing $22.96 billion in primary assets with reserve ratios above 100% for all reported assets: BTC at 111%, ETH at 101%, USDT at 106%, USDC at 101%, XRP at 108%, SOL at 104%, and OKB at 100%.

The system uses zk-STARK (zero-knowledge scalable transparent arguments of knowledge) to prove three things: that the total assets OKX claims to hold match the sum of all user balances, that no account has negative net equity, and that every user's balance is included in the verification set. Users can download the open-source zk-STARK Validator from OKX's GitHub and verify their own inclusion in the liability set independently.

This is one of the more credible PoR setups in the industry. Monthly cadence, public wallet addresses with signed "I am an OKX address" messages, zk-STARK-based verification, and open-source tooling all compare favorably to what most exchanges publish. Hacken has independently reviewed the PoR system and validated the 1:1 reserve ratio and wallet ownership claims.

What PoR does **not** prove: it's a point-in-time snapshot, not a continuous guarantee. It doesn't cover off-balance-sheet liabilities, it doesn't guarantee future withdrawal access, and it's not a full financial audit. A reserve ratio above 100% on reported assets doesn't rule out issues outside the reported scope. Treat it as strong evidence of current solvency, not as insurance against every possible failure mode.
